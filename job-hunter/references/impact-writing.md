# Impact-first recruiter writing

Recruiters need to understand why the work mattered, not only what technology
was used. Translate verified project evidence with this sequence:

`problem → intervention → outcome → evidence`

## Build the impact record

For each project or role, identify:

1. **Beneficiary** — customer, internal team, operator, or business.
2. **Problem** — slow, manual, error-prone, confusing, or blocked work.
3. **Previous workflow** — what people had to do before.
4. **Intervention** — what the candidate built or changed.
5. **Outcome** — time returned, faster delivery, fewer errors, lower risk,
   increased capacity, or better customer experience.
6. **Evidence** — measurement, artifact, repository, user confirmation,
   testimonial, or operational record.
7. **Capabilities** — technologies and judgment that produced the outcome.

Technology supports the story; it is not the story.

## Match wording to evidence

Use the strongest wording the evidence supports—never stronger.

| Confidence | Evidence | Allowed wording |
|---|---|---|
| Measured | Before/after timing, volume, error rate, or tracked result | “Reduced X from A to B” |
| Attributable | Deployed workflow plus direct operational evidence | “Enabled,” “automated,” “eliminated” |
| User-reported | Candidate or user describes the workflow benefit; no independent measurement | “Helped,” “reduced manual steps,” with attribution when needed |
| Intended | Prototype or design goal without outcome proof | “Designed to,” “aimed to,” “targeted” |

Never convert a baseline into claimed savings. If manual editing usually takes
1–3 hours but the new workflow has not been timed end to end, write “targeted a
1–3 hour manual workflow,” not “saved 1–3 hours.”

Do not claim “customers were happier” without feedback, satisfaction data, or a
credible testimonial. Use “helped deliver corrected invoices faster” or
“designed to shorten delivery time” when that is what the evidence supports.

## Drafting rules

- Lead summaries and bullets with the beneficiary and outcome.
- Name the old workflow when it makes the improvement concrete.
- Use one strong project result before listing several technologies.
- Put repository and implementation detail after the impact statement.
- Prefer plain business language over agent or AI jargon.
- Keep a missing metric visible as an evidence gap for later measurement.
- Tailor the impact to the employer's problem without changing the facts.

Bullet pattern:

> Built **[intervention]** for **[beneficiary]**, replacing **[old workflow]**
> and **[verified outcome]**; implemented with **[relevant technology]**.

## Worked examples

### Invoice correction automation

Evidence currently supports:

> Built a one-command PDF invoice-correction tool that replaces Customer
> Service's manual word-processor workflow, helping the team return corrected
> invoices faster while preserving the original file.

Supporting proof could include the repository, one-command address correction,
and documented non-destructive output. Add a before/after completion time or
correction volume when measured.

### Video editing prototype

When current evidence covers only an early instruction-driven workflow, use
prototype-strength language:

> Developed an agent-guided vertical-video editing workflow designed to reduce
> manual timeline work and give marketing teams more time for campaign work,
> using reviewable cut proposals, captions, and reproducible cut lists.

The candidate reports that manual editing can take 1–3 hours. Do not claim that
the full 1–3 hours was saved until a clean-room end-to-end run measures the new
completion time. Do not claim faster customer delivery or higher satisfaction
until delivery records or feedback verify it.
