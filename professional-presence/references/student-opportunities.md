# Student opportunities

Use this workflow for free learning resources, courses, AI-for-student programs,
scholarships, credits, vouchers, trials, and requests for free educational access.

## 1. Verify the offer

Treat LinkedIn as discovery, not proof. Open the provider's official page and
confirm:

- provider and official URL;
- what is actually free and for how long;
- deadline, geography, age, school, and student-status eligibility;
- required documents and personal fields;
- card requirement, auto-renewal, taxes, fees, or cancellation terms;
- usage limits, data-use terms, and whether the offer is still active.

Reject or flag referral-only posts, shortened or mismatched domains, requests for
credentials or payment by message, unverifiable representatives, crypto/payment
requests, unrealistic guarantees, and offers without official terms.

## 2. Show an opportunity packet

For each candidate, show:

- opportunity and provider;
- LinkedIn discovery URL and official application URL;
- practical benefit to Pon;
- verified price, limits, deadline, and eligibility;
- required personal data or documents;
- credibility or risk notes;
- recommendation: `Apply`, `Ask for access`, `Watch`, or `Skip`.

Wait for Pon to select an opportunity before preparing an application.

## 3. Prepare and fill a form

Use only truthful, relevant facts from Pon-approved sources. Never infer a missing
field or claim eligibility. Ask for missing required information one field at a
time when practical.

Before browser entry, show a field-value preview. Mask sensitive values. While Pon
watches, fill only the approved fields and stop at the final submit control. If the
form changes, requests an ID upload, introduces payment, or adds consent, show the
new requirement and wait for a new decision.

Submit only after Pon says `Submit this exact form`. Capture a non-sensitive
confirmation or application reference afterward. Never store passwords, MFA,
payment data, full IDs, or unredacted identity documents.

## 4. Ask for free access by email

Use an official support, education, community, or program contact. Confirm the
recipient belongs to the provider. Draft a short, truthful request:

```text
Subject: Student access request for [program/product]

Hello [team/name],

I'm a student interested in using [program/product] to learn [specific subject]
and build [brief public-safe purpose]. I found [offer/program] at [official URL].

Could you let me know whether free student or educational access is available,
and what proof of eligibility you require?

Thank you,
Pon
```

Tailor the message with only necessary public-safe facts. Do not mention a private
employer, customer, workplace project, financial hardship, or personal history
unless Pon explicitly asks and approves the exact disclosure.

Show the recipient, subject, body, attachments, and disclosure check. Drafting is
not sending. Send only after Pon says `Send this exact email` while watching, then
record the non-sensitive status privately.
